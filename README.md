# FINAL-SUN MK1 v1.0

**An independently designed and fabricated electromechanical flame-projection prototype.**

![FINAL-SUN MK1 v1.0 prototype, showing the integrated mechanical assembly](assets/assembly-side.jpg)

## Overview

FINAL-SUN MK1 v1.0 is a personal engineering project developed from an original system concept into a completed physical prototype. It brings together a rechargeable electrical supply, electronic controls, high-voltage arc ignition, pressurized-fuel hardware, and custom mechanical structures in a handheld assembly.

I designed the system architecture and its electrical/mechanical integration, created the original concept schematic, fabricated the custom support and housing pieces, and assembled and tested the prototype. The project was undertaken as a hands-on exercise in multidisciplinary design, fabrication, and system integration.

**Version 1.0 is the first and currently intended final iteration.** An outdoor test demonstrated the primary flame-projection function; quantitative performance and formal safety certification are not claimed.

## Design and engineering work

### System architecture

The original project concept was organized into interconnected but distinct mechanical, fuel-handling, electrical-power, control, and ignition subsystems. Developing this architecture included determining how the major components would be physically mounted, powered, and managed within one compact assembly.

### Electrical integration

A rechargeable 12 V battery supplies the electrical hardware. The prototype includes a master power switch, a battery-status display, distinct operator controls, electronically actuated fuel-control hardware, and an electric-arc ignition subsystem. The emphasis was on integrating these components into a coherent physical prototype rather than treating them as separate demonstrations.

### Mechanical design and fabrication

The assembly uses custom-designed, 3D-printed supports and housings to locate the fuel, electrical, and control components around a metal outlet structure. The visible prototype also incorporates a handheld form factor and a mounted battery assembly.

### Thermal separation feature

A **heat-resistant shield** was added between the nozzle assembly and the handle housing that contains the two control buttons. Its purpose is to create physical separation between a potential heat source and the operator-control area.

This is a design feature, **not a verified temperature or safety rating**. The shield material, thermal properties, and effectiveness under sustained operating conditions have not been documented through instrumented testing.

### Prototype validation

The project progressed through assembly and integration to an outdoor functional demonstration. Photographs document both the completed hardware and flame production during one test. No measured flame length, fuel flow, continuous-duty rating, or repeatability figures are claimed.

## Legal status and disclaimer

> **Legal notice (reviewed October 18, 2026):** This section is general information, **not legal advice or a determination that FINAL-SUN MK1 v1.0 can lawfully be manufactured, possessed, transported, or operated in any particular location**. Laws and regulations differ by jurisdiction and may change. **Legal possession does not necessarily mean legal use.**

- **United States — federal:** Privately owned flame-projection devices are not necessarily classified as *firearms* under federal firearms statutes merely because they emit flame. However, it would be inaccurate to claim that *no* federal regulation can apply: the U.S. Munitions Import List expressly addresses **military flamethrowers and projectors** under federal import controls. Device classification, commerce, import/export, and other circumstances may change the applicable rules. See [27 CFR § 447.21 — U.S. Munitions Import List](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/27/447.21) (Category II).
- **Maryland:** Maryland law expressly includes **flamethrowers** within its definition of **destructive devices** and generally prohibits their manufacture, transportation, possession, storage, sale, and use, subject to statutory exceptions. See [Maryland Criminal Law § 4-501](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcr&section=4-501), [§ 4-502](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcr&section=4-502), and [§ 4-503](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcr&section=4-503).
- **California:** State law requires a **State Fire Marshal permit** to possess or use a device that meets its legal definition of a *flamethrowing device*. That definition specifically refers to a **burning stream of combustible or flammable liquid** projected **at least 10 feet**. Its applicability to this propane-based prototype has **not** been determined here. See [California Health and Safety Code § 12750](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-hsc/division-11/part-3/chapter-1/section-12750/) and [§ 12755](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-hsc/division-11/part-3/chapter-2/section-12755/).
- **Texas, Florida, and other U.S. jurisdictions:** The absence of a general statewide flamethrower-specific prohibition should **not** be interpreted as unrestricted permission to operate such a device. Local ordinances, fire codes, wildfire restrictions, and outdoor-burning rules may apply. For example, see [Texas Local Government Code § 352.081](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=LG&Value=352.081) and [Florida Forest Service burn authorization guidance](https://www.fdacs.gov/Forest-Wildfire/Wildland-Fire/Burn-Authorizations).
- **Canada:** A blanket statement that flamethrowers are legal throughout Canada has **not been verified**. Applicable criminal, weapons, fire-safety, provincial, and municipal laws must be assessed. For example, [Criminal Code § 88](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-46/section-88.html) prohibits possession of a weapon for a purpose dangerous to the public peace or for committing an offence.

The frequently repeated shorthand that flamethrowers are “legal to own and use in 48 U.S. states” is **not adopted as a legal conclusion in this repository**. Anyone assessing a particular device or activity must confirm current requirements with the relevant authorities and, where appropriate, a qualified attorney. The existence of this project or its demonstration must not be construed as a representation of legal compliance.

## Safety and documentation scope

**This repository documents a completed personal engineering prototype. It is not a construction guide, operating manual, or endorsement of replication or use.**

Pressurized flammable gas, open flame, hot metal surfaces, electrical energy, and high-voltage ignition create serious risks of injury, fire, and property damage. The addition of a thermal shield does not establish that adjacent surfaces or controls remain at safe temperatures. Demonstrated functionality does not establish that the device is safe, compliant with applicable regulations, or suitable for repeated operation.

Detailed construction instructions, electrical wiring diagrams, fuel-routing diagrams, and operating procedures are intentionally not included.
